F1 drivers will be allowed no more than 27 laps on any set of tyres at the Qatar Grand Prix at the Lusail International Circuit next weekend.

Tyre supplier Pirelli brought in the limit to protect its tyres on the fast, demanding track. It means every team must make at least two pit stops in the 57-lap race.

## Two more laps than last year

The cap is two laps longer than the 25-lap limit used in Qatar in 2025. Pirelli said this year's cars put lower loads through the tyres than last year's, and that it has strengthened the tyre construction.

It is the third time in four years that Pirelli has set a limit at Lusail. Its long, fast corners and flat kerbs wear the tyres hard, especially the fronts.

Pirelli called the cap a "precautionary measure" linked to the circuit's unique demands.

## How the laps are counted

The limit covers each set of tyres across the whole weekend, not only the race. Laps done on a set in practice or qualifying count towards its 27.

Laps behind the safety car or virtual safety car count too. Formation laps, the laps to the grid and laps after the chequered flag do not.

## The lesson of 2025

The lap limit decided last year's race. McLaren kept Oscar Piastri and Lando Norris out when the safety car came out on lap 7, while most of the field pitted.

Both McLarens then had to stop later, and Max Verstappen won for Red Bull.

## What happens next

The Qatar Grand Prix runs from Friday 27 November to Sunday 29 November. There is no sprint race this year.

It is the second to last round of the season, before the finale in Abu Dhabi from 4 to 6 December.
