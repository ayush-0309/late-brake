Istanbul Park is doubling the size of its paddock and covering most of its grandstands before F1 returns to Turkey next year.

The Turkish Grand Prix comes back on 3 October 2027, the first race of a five-year deal that keeps Istanbul Park on the calendar until 2031.

## A fan circuit as well

The track layout stays the same, but almost everything around it is changing. The paddock is being expanded to double its capacity, with its buildings and services rebuilt from scratch.

Most of the grandstands will be covered and large fan zones added. Organisers also plan better hospitality, including at Turn 8, the circuit's famous long, multi-apex left-hander.

Eren Üçlertoprağı, president of the Turkish motorsport federation TOSFED, told Motorsport.com the aim is to keep a drivers' track and "now we're trying to make it a fan circuit as well".

## New roads, same surface

New roads in and out of the circuit have been built so traffic can arrive and leave on separate routes.

The track surface will not be relaid. Istanbul Park was resurfaced shortly before F1's 2020 visit, and the new asphalt left the cars with very little grip that weekend.

## Who runs it now

TOSFED has taken over the running of the circuit from a private company and acts as the race promoter. F1's deal, signed in April, is with Turkey's Ministry of Youth and Sports, with TOSFED as its delivery partner.

Turkey has held nine Grands Prix: seven from 2005 to 2011, then two in 2020 and 2021. Felipe Massa won three in a row for Ferrari from 2006 to 2008, and Valtteri Bottas won the last one for Mercedes.

## Race it on Late Brake

Istanbul Park isn't in Late Brake, but six real circuits are, including Interlagos, where Massa won his home race in 2006 and 2008. Pick one, set a lap, then take on AI rivals or the daily challenge.
