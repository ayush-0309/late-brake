A French favourite was born and a new country joined the F1 calendar on 21 November.

## 1943: Jacques Laffite is born in Paris

Jacques Laffite was born in Paris on 21 November 1943. He made his F1 debut at the 1974 German Grand Prix and raced until 1986.

He drove for Frank Williams' team and then Williams, but is best known for Ligier. His first win came at the 1977 Swedish Grand Prix, and he took six of the French team's nine victories.

Laffite finished his career with 6 wins, 32 podiums and 7 pole positions. He broke both legs in a crash at the start of the 1986 British Grand Prix at Brands Hatch, and never raced in F1 again.

That race was his 176th start, which matched Graham Hill's record for the most Grand Prix starts at the time.

## 2021: Hamilton wins the first Qatar Grand Prix

Lewis Hamilton won the first Qatar Grand Prix at the Losail circuit, leading all 57 laps for Mercedes. The race had been added to the calendar to replace the cancelled Australian Grand Prix.

Max Verstappen qualified second but took a five-place grid penalty for ignoring double yellow flags. He recovered to second and set the fastest lap, which stopped Hamilton taking a grand slam (pole, win, fastest lap and every lap led).

Fernando Alonso finished third for Alpine. It was his first podium since the 2014 Hungarian Grand Prix.

There were four front-left tyre failures as teams tried to make one pit stop last, against Pirelli's advice. Losail became the 30th different circuit where Hamilton had won a Grand Prix.
