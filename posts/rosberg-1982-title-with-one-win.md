This weekend F1 races in Las Vegas. The last time it raced there before the modern era, in 1982, a Finn with one win all year left as world champion.

Keke Rosberg's title remains one of the strangest in the sport's history.

## A seat at Williams

Rosberg had spent two seasons with the small Fittipaldi team. When the team's 1980 champion Alan Jones retired, Williams signed him for 1982.

It was a season like no other. Eleven different drivers from seven teams won races, and nobody won more than twice.

It was also a season of tragedy. Ferrari's Gilles Villeneuve was killed in qualifying for the Belgian Grand Prix, and his team-mate Didier Pironi suffered career-ending injuries in qualifying in Germany. Pironi missed the last five rounds.

## One win, in France

Rosberg kept collecting points without winning. His breakthrough came at the Swiss Grand Prix on 29 August, which was held at Dijon-Prenois in France because Switzerland had banned motor racing after the 1955 Le Mans disaster.

Alain Prost started from pole for Renault, but Rosberg won by 4.442 seconds. The race even ran a lap long: the chequered flag was shown after 81 laps instead of 80.

It would be his only win of the season.

## The car park showdown

The final round was the Caesars Palace Grand Prix on 25 September, on a 3.650 kilometre track laid out in a hotel car park on the Las Vegas Strip.

Rosberg arrived on 42 points to McLaren's John Watson on 33. Sixth place would be enough.

Michele Alboreto won for Tyrrell, his first F1 victory, with Watson second. Rosberg came home fifth, 1:11.375 behind the winner, and the title was his.

## Champion by five points

Rosberg finished on 44 points, five clear of Pironi and Watson on 39. He had six podiums, but just one win.

That equalled Mike Hawthorn's 1958 record for the fewest wins by a world champion. Rosberg was also the first champion from Finland.

He went on to win five Grands Prix in all. In 1985 he took pole at Silverstone with a lap averaging 259.01 km/h (160.94 mph), which stood as F1's fastest lap of any circuit until 2002. His son Nico became world champion in 2016.
