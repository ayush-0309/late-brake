The Qatar Grand Prix starts at 9:30pm India time on Sunday 29 November, under the floodlights at the Lusail International Circuit.

Qatar is two and a half hours behind India, so the race begins at 7pm local time. It is a standard weekend with no sprint race.

## Full schedule in India time

- **Practice 1:** Friday 27 November, 7:00pm to 8:00pm
- **Practice 2:** Friday 27 November, 10:30pm to 11:30pm
- **Practice 3:** Saturday 28 November, 8:00pm to 9:00pm
- **Qualifying:** Saturday 28 November, 11:30pm to 12:30am
- **Race:** Sunday 29 November, 9:30pm

No session starts after midnight in India, but qualifying runs past it and finishes at 12:30am on Sunday.

## What's at stake

Kimi Antonelli and Mercedes have already won both titles, so Qatar is about the places behind them. It is the penultimate round, with the season ending in Abu Dhabi a week later.

Max Verstappen is second in the drivers' standings on 304 points after his win in Las Vegas. Lewis Hamilton has 267, and George Russell and Lando Norris have 264 each. With 50 points left, all four can still finish runner-up.

Ferrari are second in the constructors' standings on 517 points. Red Bull, on 440, lead McLaren by nine points in the fight for third.

## The circuit

The race runs over 57 laps of the 5.419 kilometre Lusail circuit, a distance of 308.611 kilometres. Pirelli has limited each set of tyres to 27 laps, so the 57 laps need at least two pit stops.

## Race it on Late Brake

Lusail isn't in Late Brake, but six real circuits are: Spielberg, Monza, Silverstone, Spa-Francorchamps, Monaco and Interlagos. Before the cars head out under the Qatar lights, set a lap at Silverstone with the cockpit camera, then race your own ghost to see how much time you can find.
