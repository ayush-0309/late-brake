The Canadian Grand Prix has been held in Montreal since 1978, and its island circuit has produced some of F1's strangest days.

## 1. The circuit sits on a man-made island

The Circuit Gilles Villeneuve runs around Notre Dame Island in the St Lawrence River, most of which was built up for the Expo 67 world's fair.

Almost half the lap runs beside the Olympic Basin, created for the rowing and canoeing events of the 1976 Summer Olympics. Between races, the track is open to walkers, runners and cyclists.

## 2. A home winner in its first race, then its name

The first Grand Prix on the island, in 1978, was won by local hero Gilles Villeneuve for Ferrari. It was his first F1 victory and the first by a Canadian driver.

Villeneuve died in 1982, and the circuit was renamed in his honour that year.

## 3. Mansell stopped half a lap from victory

In 1991, Nigel Mansell was leading by almost a minute for Williams on the final lap when his car suddenly stopped halfway round.

Nelson Piquet, his old rival, swept past in a Benetton to take the 23rd and last win of his career.

## 4. One wall caught three world champions

The wall on the outside of the final chicane once carried the words "Bienvenue au Québec". In the 1999 race it ended the day for three world champions: Damon Hill, Michael Schumacher and Jacques Villeneuve.

It has been called the Wall of Champions ever since. Jenson Button hit it in 2005, and Sebastian Vettel did so in practice in 2011.

## 5. A first and only win a year after a huge crash

Robert Kubica crashed heavily at Montreal in 2007. Before the 2008 race, the wall he hit was moved closer to the track to reduce the angle of impact.

He came back a year later and won, with BMW Sauber team-mate Nick Heidfeld second. It was Kubica's only F1 win and the team's only victory.

## 6. The longest race in F1 history

The 2011 race took four hours, four minutes and 39 seconds, including a two-hour stoppage for torrential rain. The safety car came out six times, a record.

Jenson Button won it for McLaren despite falling to last place after a collision on lap 37. He made five pit stops and served a drive-through penalty, then passed Vettel for the lead on the final lap.

## Race it on Late Brake

Montreal isn't in Late Brake, but six real circuits are: Spielberg, Monza, Silverstone, Spa-Francorchamps, Monaco and Interlagos. Monaco has walls just as close, so try the cockpit camera there and see if you can chase down a gold medal time without touching the barriers.
