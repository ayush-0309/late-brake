Michael Schumacher won 91 Grands Prix and seven titles. His first F1 race did not last a single lap.

It happened at Spa-Francorchamps in August 1991, and he only got the chance because another driver was in prison.

## A seat comes free

Jordan was in its first F1 season in 1991. One of its drivers, Bertrand Gachot, was jailed in England after a fight with a London taxi driver, leaving a car empty for the Belgian Grand Prix.

Schumacher, then still under contract to Mercedes, impressed the team in a test at Silverstone. Mercedes paid Jordan $150,000 for his debut, and he took over car number 32.

His manager told Jordan he knew Spa well. In fact he had only watched races there, so he rode a folding bicycle around the almost 7 kilometre circuit to learn it.

## Seventh on the grid

Schumacher qualified seventh, which matched Jordan's best grid position of the year. He outqualified his experienced team-mate Andrea de Cesaris.

His race on 25 August ended on the opening lap. The clutch failed just after Eau Rouge, the steep uphill sweep after the first corner.

Ayrton Senna won for McLaren from pole, ahead of team-mate Gerhard Berger and Benetton's Nelson Piquet. De Cesaris ran second in the Jordan until his engine failed three laps from the end.

## Gone after one race

Benetton moved fast and signed Schumacher for the next race, the Italian Grand Prix at Monza.

Jordan went to the British courts to stop him, but lost because no final contract had been signed. Benetton's Roberto Moreno moved to Jordan in his place.

On 8 September, in only his second race, Schumacher started seventh and finished fifth at Monza.

## Back to Spa a year later

Schumacher returned to Spa with Benetton for the 1992 Belgian Grand Prix on 30 August. Rain fell during the race, and he started third and won by 36.595 seconds from Nigel Mansell's Williams.

It was his first Grand Prix win, in his 18th race, a year after his debut there. He went on to win the Belgian Grand Prix a record six times.
