With Cadillac confirming its 2027 line-up this week, here are six true stories from the American teams that came before it.

## 1. A win in his own car a week after Le Mans

Dan Gurney won the 1967 Belgian Grand Prix at Spa on 18 June in an Eagle, the car built by his own team. A week earlier he had won the Le Mans 24 Hours with A.J. Foyt.

He beat Jackie Stewart's BRM by more than a minute. Gurney is one of only three drivers, with Jack Brabham and Bruce McLaren, to win an F1 race in a car of his own making.

## 2. Champagne started with him

On the Le Mans podium that June, Gurney sprayed his champagne instead of drinking it, a reply to critics who had said his car would break.

He is credited with starting the champagne spray that winners still use today.

## 3. A first win that cost a beard

John Watson won the 1976 Austrian Grand Prix at the Österreichring in Spielberg for the American Penske team. It was his first F1 win and the only one Penske ever scored.

Afterwards he shaved off his beard, settling a bet with team owner Roger Penske.

## 4. Two Haas teams, two different Haases

Long before today's Haas team, an American team called Haas Lola raced in 1985 and 1986. It was owned by Carl Haas, not Gene Haas, who founded the current team.

It scored six points in all. Its best result was fourth place for Alan Jones at the 1986 Austrian Grand Prix.

## 5. Points on the first attempt

Gene Haas's team scored points in its very first race. Romain Grosjean finished sixth at the 2016 Australian Grand Prix to take eight points.

It was the first new team to score on its debut since Toyota in 2002.

## 6. A team that never turned a wheel

US F1 was given an entry for 2010 and set up its base in Charlotte, North Carolina. It even announced a driver, Argentina's José María López.

The money ran out before the season began. The team was taken off the entry list on 3 March 2010 and never raced.

## Race it on Late Brake

Spa-Francorchamps, where Gurney won, and Spielberg, home of Penske's only victory, are both among the six real circuits in Late Brake. Drive Spa with the racing line on, then switch it off and chase the gold medal time.
