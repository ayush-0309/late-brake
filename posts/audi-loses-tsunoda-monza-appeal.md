Audi has lost its appeal against Yuki Tsunoda's result at the Italian Grand Prix, after the FIA's International Court of Appeal ruled on Friday.

The court found the appeal admissible but dismissed it on the merits, so Tsunoda keeps 10th place and the last point from Monza.

## What Audi wanted

The race on 6 September was red-flagged early on after a heavy crash for Ferrari's Charles Leclerc, then restarted from the grid.

Before the restart, Tsunoda made a late move into his grid slot and the race director ordered an extra formation lap. The rules say a driver who causes an extra formation lap must start from the pit lane.

Tsunoda started from the grid as normal and the stewards took no further action. Audi argued he should have been penalised, which could have moved its own drivers up the order.

## Why the stewards' decision stands

The court upheld the stewards' original ruling, and its full reasons will be published later.

The stewards had said the penalty is for failing to start from the pit lane, not for causing the extra lap itself. Neither Tsunoda nor his team was told they were seen as the cause, so they had no reason to send him to the pit lane.

The car had also stopped correctly in its grid box.

## What it means for Audi

Audi's Gabriel Bortoleto and Nico Hülkenberg finished 11th and 12th at Monza, right behind Tsunoda, so neither gains a point.

That matters because Audi is fighting Haas for seventh place in the constructors' championship, with two rounds left: this weekend's Qatar Grand Prix and the Abu Dhabi finale.
