In September 2008, the smallest team on the grid beat Ferrari and McLaren at Monza, in front of Ferrari's home crowd.

The team was Toro Rosso, Red Bull's junior outfit, built from the old Minardi squad. Its driver was a 21-year-old German called Sebastian Vettel.

## Pole in the rain

Rain soaked Monza on the Saturday of the Italian Grand Prix, and Vettel mastered it. He took pole with a lap of 1:37.555, becoming the youngest pole-sitter F1 had seen.

McLaren's Heikki Kovalainen joined him on the front row. Red Bull's Mark Webber was third, and Vettel's team-mate Sébastien Bourdais fourth, so both Toro Rossos started inside the top four.

The title contenders struggled. Kimi Räikkönen qualified 14th for Ferrari and Lewis Hamilton 15th for McLaren, the first time Hamilton had failed to reach the final part of qualifying.

## Away behind the safety car

Heavy rain before the start meant the race began behind the safety car. Bourdais stalled on the grid and had to start from the back, a lap down.

When the safety car pulled in at the end of lap 2, Vettel simply drove away. By lap 8 he was 6.3 seconds clear of Kovalainen, and the gap grew as the track slowly dried.

He made his pit stop on lap 18 and stayed in front through a brief shower on lap 26, while most of the field switched to intermediate tyres as the track dried.

## A record and a first

Vettel won by 12.512 seconds from Kovalainen, with BMW Sauber's Robert Kubica third.

At 21 years and 73 days, he became the youngest winner in F1 history, beating the record Fernando Alonso had set in Hungary in 2003. Max Verstappen took it from him at the 2016 Spanish Grand Prix.

It was Toro Rosso's first win, and many of the staff who made it happen had worked for Minardi, a team that never won a race. It was also the first win for an Italian-based team other than Ferrari since 1957.

## What it led to

Behind the winner, the title fight tightened. Felipe Massa finished sixth and Hamilton seventh, leaving Hamilton just one point ahead, 78 to 77, with four races to go.

Vettel moved up to the senior Red Bull team for 2009 and won four world titles there. The Faenza team still races today, now as Racing Bulls.
