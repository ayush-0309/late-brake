Williams has stopped bringing upgrades to its 2026 F1 car and moved its development work to next year's car, with three races of the season left.

The team has found extra performance in its wind tunnel but chosen not to build the parts, saving its money under the cost cap for 2027.

## Performance left in the tunnel

Team principal James Vowles has admitted the decision hurts. He said Williams had more aerodynamic gains ready in the wind tunnel but chose not to bring them to the track, keeping the money for next year instead.

The Race reports that the team's simulator, wind tunnel and computer modelling work have all moved on to the 2027 car. Williams is also running with few spare parts and using components for as long as it can, so it does not pay for pieces that will soon be out of date.

## Practice as a test session

Williams now uses race weekends to gather data for 2027, even if that costs it pace on the day. In first practice at the United States Grand Prix, its two drivers spent most of the session on short test runs rather than chasing lap times.

The aim is to check how the real car behaves against the team's simulator and computer models, so that next year's car performs as predicted.

## A difficult first year of the new rules

Williams hoped to carry its 2025 form into the new 2026 rules. Instead, its car arrived late and overweight, and it has struggled for pace.

A new chassis introduced at the Azerbaijan Grand Prix in September brought the car down to the weight limit. That chassis carries over to 2027, so the work was largely an investment in next season.

The team sits ninth of 11 in the constructors' championship with 12 points, ahead of only Aston Martin and Cadillac. The season continues in Las Vegas this weekend, then Qatar and Abu Dhabi.
