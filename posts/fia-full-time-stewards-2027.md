F1 will have full-time race stewards for the first time from 2027, the FIA announced on Thursday. The governing body will hire a small first group next season and grow it each year until it reaches 12 to 15 stewards.

The stewards are the officials who decide penalties during a Grand Prix weekend. Drivers and team bosses have asked for full-time stewards for years, mainly because rulings have changed from one race to the next.

## How the change works

Today each race has its own panel, picked from a large rotating pool. It mixes FIA appointees, people put forward by the local motorsport authority and one former driver, and the work is part-time.

The first full-time stewards will be recruited during the 2027 season. More will follow from 2028, and they will sit on panels alongside part-time stewards and the former driver, known as the driver steward, for the foreseeable future.

## More than race weekends

The FIA's main aim is more consistent decisions. Between races, the full-time stewards will also look for new trends in driving, point out rules that need clearer wording and help train the next generation of officials.

FIA president Mohammed Ben Sulayem called the recruitment "a significant step". It follows the opening this year of the FIA's remote race control centre at Biggin Hill in England.

## What happens next

The change starts in 2027, so the last three races of 2026 keep the current panels. The first of them is the Las Vegas Grand Prix, which starts at 9:30am India time on Sunday 22 November, with qualifying at 9:30am India time on Saturday.
