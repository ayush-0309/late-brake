This weekend F1 races on the Las Vegas Strip. The first time it came to the Strip, in 1981, the world title was settled in a hotel car park by a driver who could barely finish.

## Three drivers, one title

The Caesars Palace Grand Prix on 17 October 1981 was the last of 15 rounds. Williams' Carlos Reutemann led the championship on 49 points.

Brabham's Nelson Piquet was one point behind on 48, and Ligier's Jacques Laffite had 43. Reutemann's team-mate, reigning champion Alan Jones, was already out of the running.

## A circuit in a car park

The 3.650 kilometre track was laid out in the car park of the Caesars Palace hotel. It ran anticlockwise, which put unfamiliar strain on drivers' necks, and many disliked its flat, repetitive layout.

Reutemann took pole with a lap of 1:17.821, 0.174 seconds quicker than Jones. Piquet, who had been unwell during practice, qualified fourth.

## Reutemann fades

Jones led from the start and was five seconds clear after two laps. Reutemann dropped to fifth on the opening lap, and his Williams soon lost fourth gear.

On lap 17 Piquet passed him on the inside. Reutemann kept slipping back and finished eighth, a lap down, without a point.

Jones won the 75-lap race by 20.048 seconds from Renault's Alain Prost, with Bruno Giacomelli third for Alfa Romeo. It was the last F1 win for Jones, who had announced his retirement, and the last by an Australian until Mark Webber won the 2009 German Grand Prix.

## Piquet hangs on

The heat took its toll on Piquet. Nigel Mansell passed him for fourth, but Piquet held on to fifth, the place he needed, 1 minute 16.438 seconds behind Jones.

Laffite snatched sixth from John Watson at the last corner, but his 44 points were not enough. Piquet was champion with 50 points to Reutemann's 49.

He needed about 15 minutes to recover from heat exhaustion after the finish. It was his first world title, and Williams took the constructors' crown with 95 points.

F1 returned to Caesars Palace once more, in 1982, then stayed away from Las Vegas for 41 years.
