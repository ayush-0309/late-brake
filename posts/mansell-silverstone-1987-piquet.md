Two Williams team-mates who were bitter rivals, one home crowd and a gap of almost half a minute. The 1987 British Grand Prix at Silverstone became one of the best-loved wins in F1.

## Two Williams drivers, one fight

In 1987 the Williams-Honda was the car to beat, and its two drivers were fierce rivals: Nelson Piquet of Brazil, already a two-time champion, and Nigel Mansell, the home hero.

At Silverstone on 12 July, Piquet took pole position with a lap of 1:07.110. Mansell was alongside him, just 0.070 seconds slower.

Alain Prost's McLaren led away, but Piquet passed him at Maggotts and Mansell soon followed. From then on it was a private Williams duel, with Piquet in front.

## A gamble on fresh tyres

Both Williams drivers had planned to run the 65 laps without changing tyres. On lap 35, Mansell was about two seconds behind Piquet when he and the team decided he should stop for fresh tyres.

He rejoined nearly half a minute behind, with 28 laps to go.

Mansell then went on the attack, breaking the lap record eight times as he cut into the gap. His best, a 1:09.832 on lap 58, was the fastest lap of the race.

## The dummy on the Hangar Straight

Lap by lap, Piquet's lead melted away. On lap 63, with two to go, Mansell sold his team-mate a dummy on the Hangar Straight and swept into the lead.

He won by 1.918 seconds. Ayrton Senna's Lotus was third, a lap down, and Satoru Nakajima made it four Honda-powered cars in the top four.

On the slowing-down lap, Mansell's car stopped and was engulfed by fans who had run onto the track. It was first thought he had run out of fuel, but he had used extra engine power for the closing laps and the engine had failed.

It was his second British Grand Prix win in a row.

## How the season ended

The win put Mansell level with Piquet on 30 points, one behind Senna. But in qualifying for the Japanese Grand Prix at Suzuka, Mansell crashed and injured his back.

He missed the last two races of the season, and Piquet became world champion for the third time.
