Five cars crossed the line at Monza in 1971 separated by just 0.61 seconds. The man in front, by one hundredth of a second, never led a full lap of a Grand Prix and never won another.

Peter Gethin's victory in the Italian Grand Prix is still the closest finish in Formula 1 history.

## The fastest track in F1

In 1971 Monza had no chicanes. With Spa-Francorchamps off the calendar, it was the fastest circuit F1 raced on, and races there often broke up into high-speed packs.

Chris Amon took pole for Matra with a lap of 1:22.4, the fastest lap of any F1 championship race at the time, at 251 km/h.

Gethin, a British driver who had joined BRM from McLaren at the previous round in Austria, started 11th in the BRM P160.

## The favourites fall away

Ferrari's Clay Regazzoni charged from the fourth row to lead the opening laps. March's Ronnie Peterson took over on lap 3, and reigning champion Jackie Stewart led from lap 7.

The leaders then began to drop out. Stewart's Tyrrell and Jacky Ickx's Ferrari both retired with engine problems by lap 16, and Regazzoni followed two laps later.

That left a pack fighting for the win: Peterson, Tyrrell's François Cevert, Mike Hailwood on his debut for Surtees, Jo Siffert, Howden Ganley, Amon, Jackie Oliver and Gethin. Siffert dropped back when his gearbox would only select fourth gear.

## Fourth to first on the last lap

Five cars were still together on the final lap: Gethin, Peterson, Cevert, Hailwood and Ganley.

Gethin started that lap fourth and came out of it first. He won by 0.01 seconds from Peterson, with Cevert 0.09 seconds behind and Hailwood 0.18 seconds behind. Ganley, also driving a BRM, was fifth, 0.61 seconds back.

Timing then only measured to the nearest hundredth of a second, so the real gap may have been even smaller. Amon took sixth, nearly half a minute later.

## A record that lasted 32 years

Gethin averaged 242.615 km/h over the 55 laps. No F1 race was run faster until the 2003 Italian Grand Prix, also at Monza.

Monza added two chicanes for 1972 to slow the cars down. Gethin never stood on another championship podium, and his win at Monza remained his only Grand Prix victory before he left F1 in 1974.

## Race it on Late Brake

Monza is one of the six real circuits in Late Brake, though today's version has the chicanes Gethin never had to brake for. Race your own ghost there and see if you can find a hundredth of a second, the margin that won in 1971.
