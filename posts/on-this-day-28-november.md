Three figures from very different eras of F1 died on 28 November: two drivers from the championship's early years and the man behind one of its most successful teams.

## 1990: Paco Godia, Spain's first F1 driver

Francisco "Paco" Godia died in his home city of Barcelona, aged 69. He was the first Spaniard to race in a Formula 1 Grand Prix.

Godia started 13 Grands Prix between 1951 and 1958, all in Maseratis and mostly in the 250F. His best season was 1956, when he finished fourth in both the German and Italian Grands Prix and scored all six of his career points, enough for ninth in the championship.

## 1993: Joe Kelly, who raced in F1's first championship race

Dublin-born Joe Kelly died in Neston, Cheshire, aged 80. He took part in the very first World Championship race, the 1950 British Grand Prix at Silverstone, in his own Alta.

He returned to Silverstone for the 1951 British Grand Prix, but was not classified in either race and never scored a point. Kelly went on to set lap records at the Curragh in Ireland before a serious accident at Oulton Park in 1955 ended his full-time racing career.

## 2021: Sir Frank Williams

Frank Williams died in Frimley, Surrey, aged 79. He founded Williams Grand Prix Engineering with engineer Patrick Head in 1977, and the team's first win came two years later, when Clay Regazzoni took the 1979 British Grand Prix at Silverstone.

Under Williams the team won seven drivers' titles and nine constructors' titles. A road accident in the south of France in March 1986 left him tetraplegic, and he was knighted in 1999.

He had no further involvement with the team after it was sold in September 2020.
