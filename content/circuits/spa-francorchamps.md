## Seven kilometres through the forest

Spa-Francorchamps runs through the hills of the Ardennes in eastern Belgium, near the towns of Spa and Francorchamps.
The first races here used a triangle of public roads about 15 km long. The modern layout, opened in 1979, keeps the
best of that old circuit in a lap of about 7 km, still the longest of the season.

Its length brings its own weather. Rain can fall on one part of the lap while another stays dry, and races here have
been turned upside down by a single shower.

## Corner by corner

- **La Source.** A tight hairpin just after the start, and a classic place for first-lap trouble.
- **Eau Rouge and Raidillon.** The famous dip and steep climb: a left, a right and a left again, taken flat out by the
  bravest, with the car going light over the crest.
- **Kemmel Straight.** A long uphill run where slipstreaming rivals line up to pass.
- **Les Combes.** A right-left chicane at the end of the straight, the main overtaking spot.
- **Pouhon.** A fast, sweeping double left-hander that drops downhill.
- **Blanchimont.** A very fast left-hander near the end of the lap.
- **The final chicane.** A slow right-left before the run back to the line.

## How to be fast here

Spa is a mix of very fast corners and a few heavy stops. Carry speed through Eau Rouge and Pouhon, brake hard and
straight for Les Combes and the final chicane, and remember that one mistake costs more here than anywhere because
the lap is so long.
