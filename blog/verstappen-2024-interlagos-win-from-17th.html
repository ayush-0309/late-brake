How Verstappen won from 17th in the 2024 Interlagos rain

Max Verstappen started the 2024 F1 São Paulo Grand Prix 17th in torrential rain and won by almost 20 seconds, all but settling his fourth title.

By Ayush Garg

Rain has already shaken up qualifying for this weekend's F1 São Paulo Grand Prix, and Interlagos has a history of wet races. Two years ago Max Verstappen started 17th in a downpour there and won by almost 20 seconds.

It was one of the great wet-weather drives, and it came when his title lead was under pressure from Lando Norris.

Qualifying on race morning

Heavy rain on Saturday 2 November 2024 pushed qualifying back to 7:30am on Sunday, the first time in more than five years that F1 had qualified on race day.

The session had five red flags, equalling the record. Norris took pole for McLaren with 1m23.405s, ahead of George Russell and Yuki Tsunoda.

Verstappen qualified 12th. A five-place penalty for his sixth internal combustion engine of the year dropped him to 17th.

Through the field in the spray

The race start was brought forward to 12:30pm because of the forecast, and even then it began late. Lance Stroll spun into the gravel on the formation lap, the start was aborted and the race was cut from 71 laps to 69.

Verstappen climbed from 17th to sixth in the first 12 laps.

The red flag that decided it

On lap 28 a virtual safety car came out, and Russell and Norris pitted. That moved Alpine's Esteban Ocon, Verstappen and Pierre Gasly ahead.

Four laps later Franco Colapinto crashed heavily, and the race was red flagged. Ocon, Verstappen and Gasly fitted fresh full wet tyres during the stoppage without losing their places.

When racing resumed after a later safety car, Verstappen passed Ocon for the lead and pulled away, setting the fastest lap of the race on lap 67.

He won by 19.477 seconds from Ocon, with Gasly third for an Alpine double podium. Norris locked up at a restart and finished sixth.

The title all but settled

It was Verstappen's first win since the Spanish Grand Prix, and the first win from 17th or lower since Kimi Räikkönen's victory at the 2005 Japanese Grand Prix.

He left Brazil 62 points clear of Norris with three races to go. He clinched his fourth consecutive title in Las Vegas on 23 November, finishing fifth.
