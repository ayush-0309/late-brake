How Karun Chandhok became India's second F1 driver

From Formula Maruti in Chennai to a last-minute seat at a brand new team, this is how Karun Chandhok reached F1 in 2010, and what came after.

By Ayush Garg

Five years after Narain Karthikeyan's debut, India got its second F1 driver in 2010. Karun Chandhok's first race came in a car he had barely driven.

A racing family from Chennai

Chandhok was born in Madras, now Chennai, on 19 January 1984. His father, Vicky Chandhok, was a multiple Indian rally champion and has led the Federation of Motor Sports Clubs of India since 2003.

Karun won the Indian national title in Formula Maruti in 2000, taking seven of the ten races. A year later he was Formula 2000 Asia champion, with eight wins from 14 races.

He then raced in British Formula 3, was the first driver for A1 Team India in 2005, and won the Formula V6 Asia championship in 2006 with seven wins.

Two wins in GP2

In 2007 Chandhok moved up to GP2, the series just below F1. Driving for Durango, he won the sprint race at Spa-Francorchamps and finished the year 15th.

He added a second GP2 win with iSport International in 2008 and finished tenth. In 2009, with Ocean Racing Technology, his best result was third at Silverstone.

A car finished at the last minute

HRT, a new Spanish team, named Chandhok as one of its drivers in March 2010, alongside his former GP2 team-mate Bruno Senna.

The team was so late that his car was still being built during practice for the opener in Bahrain, and he did not complete a lap in any session. He drove the HRT for the first time in qualifying and started last, 1.7 seconds behind Senna.

His race lasted barely a lap. A large bump in the track sent him into the wall.

Two weeks later in Australia, Chandhok finished 14th. It was the first time an HRT had been classified at the end of a Grand Prix.

Ten races, then a new path

He started the first ten races of 2010 without scoring a point, and HRT replaced him with Sakon Yamamoto from the German Grand Prix onwards.

In 2011 he joined Team Lotus as reserve driver. He made one more start, standing in for Jarno Trulli at the German Grand Prix and finishing 20th, which took his F1 total to 11 races.

Chandhok then turned to sports cars and finished sixth overall at the 2012 Le Mans 24 Hours with JRM. He raced for Mahindra in the first season of Formula E, and has been part of Sky Sports' F1 team since 2019.
