On this day: Hamilton wins Austin's first race, F1 returns to Vegas

On 18 November, Lewis Hamilton won the first F1 race at Austin in 2012 and Max Verstappen won F1's return to Las Vegas in 2023.

By Ayush Garg

Two new chapters for F1 in the United States opened on 18 November, 11 years apart.

2012: Hamilton wins the first race in Austin

Lewis Hamilton won the first F1 race at the Circuit of the Americas in Austin, Texas. It was also the first United States Grand Prix since 2007.

Sebastian Vettel started from pole position for Red Bull, but Hamilton passed him on lap 42 using DRS, the overtaking aid that opens a flap in the rear wing. He crossed the line 0.675 seconds ahead after 56 laps. Ferrari's Fernando Alonso finished third, 39.229 seconds behind.

Vettel set the fastest lap, a 1:39.347. With one race left, he led Alonso by 273 points to 260 in the title fight.

The win was Hamilton's last for McLaren before he moved to Mercedes.

2023: Verstappen wins on F1's return to Las Vegas

Max Verstappen won the Las Vegas Grand Prix, run late on Saturday night local time. It was F1's first race in the city since the 1982 Caesars Palace Grand Prix.

Ferrari's Charles Leclerc started from pole. Verstappen picked up a five-second penalty for forcing Leclerc off the track at the first corner, but still won the 50-lap race by 2.070 seconds.

Leclerc took second by passing Verstappen's team-mate Sergio Pérez on the final lap.

The weekend had begun badly. In first practice, Carlos Sainz's Ferrari hit a loose metal cover on the track, which damaged his car and cost him a 10-place grid penalty.
