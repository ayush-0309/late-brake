How Senna held off Mansell to win the 1992 Monaco Grand Prix

Nigel Mansell had won every race of 1992 until a late pit stop at Monaco, then spent the final laps glued to Ayrton Senna's gearbox.

By Ayush Garg

In 1992, Nigel Mansell's Williams looked unbeatable. At Monaco, Ayrton Senna beat it anyway, by 0.215 seconds.

Five from five

Mansell won the first five races of the season, from South Africa to San Marino. His Williams-Renault was the fastest car on the grid.

At Monaco on 31 May, round 6 of 16, he took pole with 1:19.495. It was his sixth pole from six races.

Team-mate Riccardo Patrese qualified second. Senna, the reigning champion, was third in his McLaren-Honda.

A late stop

Mansell led from the start. Senna passed Patrese into Sainte Dévote, the first corner, and by the end of the opening lap the order was Mansell, Senna, Patrese.

Mansell stayed in front until the closing stages of the 78-lap race. Then a suspected puncture sent him into the pits, and Senna took the lead.

Goodyear later said there had been no puncture. Williams designer Adrian Newey suggested a loose wheel nut may have been to blame.

Three laps to hold on

On fresh tyres, Mansell set the fastest lap of the race, 1:21.598 on lap 74. He cut Senna's lead from 5.2 seconds to 1.9 seconds in two laps.

Senna had run the whole race on one set of tyres. He later said it was like "driving on ice".

For the final three laps, Mansell tried everything to pass the McLaren but found no way by. He said afterwards that Senna's car had been too wide to get past.

Senna won by 0.215 seconds, with Patrese third. Mansell was so exhausted that marshals helped him up the stairs to the podium.

The record and the title

It was Senna's fifth Monaco win and his fourth in a row, equalling Graham Hill's record. He won there again in 1993 to set a new record of six.

Mansell did not need Monaco. He clinched the title at the Hungaroring on 16 August with five races to spare, and finished the year with nine wins, a record for one season at the time.
