FIA turns to Amazon's AI to help write future F1 rules

The FIA has made Amazon Web Services its official AI and cloud computing partner, with its tools set to help shape the next generation of F1 cars.

By Ayush Garg

The FIA, motor sport's governing body, has signed Amazon Web Services (AWS) as its official AI and cloud computing partner.

The deal, announced on Wednesday, means AWS's artificial intelligence tools will help the FIA write the technical rules for the next generation of Formula 1 cars.

Rules shaped in a virtual wind tunnel

The FIA already runs its aerodynamic simulations on AWS's high performance computing. That lets its engineers test how air flows over a car without building any physical parts, which saves time and money.

Aerodynamic simulation, often called CFD (computational fluid dynamics), is how teams and rule makers predict downforce and drag on a computer.

Under the new deal, the FIA's technical and engineering teams will also get AI tools to help them develop rules on safety, performance and sustainability.

FIA president Mohammed Ben Sulayem said "the FIA must lead that change" as technology reshapes the sport.

Beyond Formula 1

The partnership reaches well past the grid. AWS and the FIA launched a digital licensing platform last year for drivers, officials and volunteers, and it will now be rolled out to more national motor sport bodies.

AWS also becomes an official partner of the annual FIA Awards and of the FIA Sustainable Innovation Series.

The FIA says the deal supports its aim of doubling the number of people taking part in motor sport around the world.

A familiar name in the paddock

AWS is not new to Formula 1. It has been a technology partner of Formula 1 since 2018.

The current rules for cars and engines only arrived this season. The FIA's engineers are already looking ahead to the set that will follow them.
