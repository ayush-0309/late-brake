Six F1 British Grand Prix facts that sound made up

A shared win, a wartime airfield and a chequered flag shown a lap early: six true stories from the F1 British Grand Prix.

By Ayush Garg

The British Grand Prix is one of F1's oldest races, and it has collected some strange stories along the way.

1. The first British Grand Prix had two winners

The first race, at Brooklands on 7 August 1926, was won by Robert Sénéchal and Louis Wagner. The two drivers shared a single Delage and split the victory between them.

2. Silverstone began life as an RAF airfield

Like many British circuits built after the Second World War, Silverstone was laid out on a disused Royal Air Force airfield. Its first race, on 2 October 1948, was won by Luigi Villoresi in a Maserati.

3. Silverstone shared the race for more than 30 years

From 1955 to 1962, the British Grand Prix was shared between Silverstone and Aintree, near Liverpool. From 1963 to 1986, it alternated with Brands Hatch, with Silverstone in odd years. Silverstone has hosted every British Grand Prix since 1987.

4. Two drivers shared the first win for a British team

At Aintree in 1957, Tony Brooks felt unwell, so Stirling Moss took over his Vanwall. Brooks drove 26 laps and Moss 64, and they shared the win and its eight points. It was the first World Championship race won by a British constructor.

5. One crash took out 11 cars

On the first lap in 1973, a pile-up at Woodcote corner put 11 cars out of the race, including all three works Surtees. Nobody was killed, but Andrea de Adamich's ankle injuries ended his career.

6. The fastest qualifying lap stood for 17 years, and the race ended a lap early

In 1985, Keke Rosberg took pole in his Williams-Honda with a 1:05.591, at an average of more than 258 kilometres per hour. It stood as the fastest qualifying lap in F1 history for 17 years.

The race was strange too. The chequered flag came out a lap early, after 65 of the 66 laps, with winner Alain Prost a lap ahead of the entire field.
