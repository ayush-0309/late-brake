On this day: Schumacher's last race and Alonso's farewell

On 25 November Michael Schumacher raced for the last time at Interlagos in 2012, and Fernando Alonso and Kimi Räikkönen said goodbye in Abu Dhabi in 2018.

By Ayush Garg

Two season finales, six years apart, both ended careers on 25 November.

2012: Button wins as Schumacher bows out

The 2012 season ended at Interlagos with the Brazilian Grand Prix, run in rain that grew heavier towards the finish.

Jenson Button won for McLaren, 2.754 seconds ahead of Ferrari's Fernando Alonso, with Felipe Massa third. It was the 15th and last win of Button's F1 career. Team-mate Lewis Hamilton had started from pole.

Sebastian Vettel recovered to sixth, enough to beat Alonso to the title by three points, 281 to 278. At 25 he became the youngest driver to win three world championships.

Michael Schumacher finished seventh for Mercedes in the last race of his career. The points were the final ones he scored in F1.

2018: Hamilton wins as Alonso and Räikkönen move on

Six years later, Lewis Hamilton won the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix from pole, 2.581 seconds ahead of Ferrari's Sebastian Vettel, with Red Bull's Max Verstappen third.

Hamilton had already won his fifth world title, and he finished the season on 408 points.

It was Fernando Alonso's last race before he stepped away from F1. He finished 11th for McLaren, a lap down, then celebrated with doughnuts on the pit straight. He returned to the sport in 2021.

Kimi Räikkönen's final race for Ferrari lasted five laps: he retired on lap 6 with a loss of power, having started fourth. He moved to Sauber, racing as Alfa Romeo, for 2019.

The race was also the last overseen by race director Charlie Whiting, who died before the next race.
