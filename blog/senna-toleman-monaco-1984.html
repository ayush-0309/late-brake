How Senna nearly won the 1984 Monaco Grand Prix in a Toleman

In the rain at Monaco in 1984, rookie Ayrton Senna charged from 13th in an unfancied Toleman and was catching the leader when the race was stopped.

By Ayush Garg

Ayrton Senna went on to win at Monaco a record six times. But many fans still remember the race he didn't win there: a soaking afternoon in 1984, in a car nobody expected to be near the front.

A rookie in a small team

Senna joined Toleman for 1984, his first season in F1. The British team was a long way from the front, and he retired from his debut race in Brazil when the turbocharger on his Hart engine failed on lap 8.

Monaco, on 3 June, was the sixth of 16 rounds and his first F1 race on a street circuit. He qualified 13th.

It was the only race of the 1984 season run in the wet. The rain was so heavy that the start was delayed by 45 minutes.

Chaos in the rain

Alain Prost led from pole in his McLaren. Behind him, Renault's Derek Warwick and Patrick Tambay both crashed at the first corner, and Tambay broke his leg.

Nigel Mansell passed Prost on lap nine to lead a Grand Prix for the first time. Six laps later his Lotus slid on a painted white line on the climb to Casino Square, and he was out.

Niki Lauda, in the second McLaren, spun out on lap 23. Prost's carbon brakes were locking because they could not get hot enough in the conditions.

Senna had picked his way past car after car, including René Arnoux's Ferrari. Now he was second and catching Prost fast.

The red flag

On lap 29 Prost waved to the officials to say he thought the race should be stopped. The clerk of the course, Jacky Ickx, showed the red flag at the end of lap 32.

Senna passed the slowing McLaren before the line. But the rules took the result from the last lap every car had completed, lap 31, when Prost was still ahead.

The decision was controversial. Ickx was suspended from race control for not consulting the stewards first.

What happened next

Senna's second place was his first F1 podium, and he set the fastest lap of the race, his first in F1. He took two more podiums that season, at Brands Hatch and Estoril.

Because the race ended so early, only half points were given. Prost took 4.5 points for the win and lost the 1984 title to Lauda by half a point.

Senna won his first Monaco Grand Prix three years later, in 1987, and his sixth and last in 1993.
