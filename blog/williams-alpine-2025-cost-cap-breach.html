FIA finds Williams broke F1's 2025 cost cap by £627,000

Williams overspent F1's 2025 budget cap by 0.48% and loses some wind tunnel time, while Alpine is fined for a reporting breach.

By Ayush Garg

Williams broke Formula 1's cost cap in 2025, overspending by £627,000, the FIA said on Wednesday.

The overspend is 0.48% of the cap. Williams will pay a fine and lose 1.5% of its wind tunnel and CFD (computer simulations of airflow) testing time for the next 12 months.

A minor overspend

The FIA classed it as a minor overspend breach, the category for teams that go less than 5% over the cap. Williams also committed a procedural breach, meaning its financial reporting was wrong.

The governing body said both breaches were unintentional and came from Williams' good faith reading of the rules. It found no evidence that the team sought or gained any advantage, and said Williams cooperated throughout.

The amount of the fine was not made public. Williams agreed the penalty with the FIA's Cost Cap Administration in an Accepted Breach Agreement dated 23 November.

Company changes behind the breach

Williams said the extra spending was administrative costs from changes to its company structure, which it believed fell outside the cap. After talks with the FIA, it accepted that they should have been counted.

"Integrity is central to what Williams stands for," the team said in a statement.

Alpine fined for its reporting

Alpine stayed under the cap but committed a procedural breach. The FIA found it had wrongly left out or adjusted some costs in its 2025 accounts, including marketing and staff-related spending.

Alpine will pay an undisclosed fine and the Cost Cap Administration's costs. It signed its own agreement on 11 November. The FIA again found no sign that the team gained an advantage.

What happens next

The other nine teams were found to have kept to the 2025 rules, as were all five power unit manufacturers. The FIA reviewed reports from 11 teams, including Cadillac, which joined the grid this year.

Williams races next at the Qatar Grand Prix at Lusail, which starts at 9:30pm India time on Sunday 29 November.
