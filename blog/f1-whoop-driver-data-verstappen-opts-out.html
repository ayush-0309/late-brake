F1 to show drivers' heart rates on TV as Verstappen opts out

F1 has signed WHOOP to put drivers' heart-rate data on live broadcasts from 2027, but Max Verstappen says he will not take part.

By Ayush Garg

Formula 1 has signed the wearable fitness company WHOOP as an official partner, and will show data from drivers' wrist trackers during live TV coverage from 2027. Max Verstappen has already said he will not take part.

Drivers will wear the trackers in the car, and heart rate is the main figure F1 plans to show. Taking part is up to each driver.

How it will work

The FIA has certified the device, so drivers can now wear it openly while they race. At least a handful of drivers have signed up so far.

Ferrari already has its own deal with WHOOP. Its drivers already wore the trackers, but until now they had to keep them out of sight in the car.

Verstappen says no

Verstappen said he does not use any tracking device, even in training, and sees it as a matter of privacy. "I don't like to be tracked and I know when I'm tired," the Red Bull driver said.

He added that he has no objection if other drivers want to share their data.

Mixed views in the paddock

Ferrari's Charles Leclerc, now a WHOOP ambassador, uses the tracker for his sleep and recovery and expects to see his data on screen next year. He said he has no problem with that.

Mercedes driver George Russell is in between. He said he would enjoy the data as a fan, but warned that natural heart rates differ between people, so viewers comparing drivers could reach the wrong conclusions.

What happens next

Nothing changes on screen this season. The Las Vegas Grand Prix starts at 9:30am India time on Sunday 22 November, with qualifying at 9:30am India time on Saturday.
