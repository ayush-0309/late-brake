How Panis won the 1996 Monaco Grand Prix from 14th on the grid

Olivier Panis started 14th in a wet Monaco Grand Prix in 1996 and won for Ligier, in a race that had only three cars running at the flag.

By Ayush Garg

Twenty-two cars started the 1996 Monaco Grand Prix. Only three were still running at the end, and the one in front was a Ligier that had started 14th.

Olivier Panis's win on 19 May 1996 was the only one of his Formula 1 career, and the last the Ligier team would ever score.

A wet morning and a poor grid

Michael Schumacher took pole for Ferrari. The Ligiers had a difficult qualifying with misfires, and Panis lined up 14th, three places ahead of team-mate Pedro Diniz.

Heavy rain before the race led to an extra 15-minute warm-up session. Andrea Montermini crashed his Forti in it and could not start.

The track was wet at the start. Williams' Damon Hill passed Schumacher into Sainte-Dévote, and on the first lap Schumacher lost control at Lower Mirabeau and hit the wall.

The leaders fall away

As the track dried, the drivers switched to slick tyres. Hill pitted on lap 30, briefly lost the lead to Benetton's Jean Alesi, then took it back a lap later.

Hill pulled out a lead of nearly 30 seconds. Then, on lap 40, a failed oil pump blew his engine as he came out of the tunnel. It was his first retirement of the season.

Alesi led for the next 20 laps until his suspension failed. That put Panis in front.

How Panis got there

Panis had been climbing through the field all race. He forced his way past Eddie Irvine's Ferrari at the Loews hairpin, and a well-timed stop for slick tyres moved him further up.

Behind him, the field kept shrinking. Irvine spun late in the race at the same spot as Schumacher. As he tried to rejoin, Mika Salo's Tyrrell hit him, and Mika Häkkinen's McLaren hit Salo. All three were out.

The race was scheduled for 78 laps, but the two-hour time limit ended it after 75. Panis won by 4.828 seconds from McLaren's David Coulthard, with Johnny Herbert third for Sauber.

Heinz-Harald Frentzen pulled into the pits on the last lap, so only those three took the chequered flag. Four more drivers were classified because they had covered enough of the distance. The race still holds the record for the fewest cars running at the finish.

The last Ligier win

The victory ended a 15-year wait for Ligier, whose previous win was Jacques Laffite's at the 1981 Canadian Grand Prix. It was also the first win for Mugen-Honda engines.

It was the team's last. Ligier was sold to Alain Prost in February 1997 and became Prost Grand Prix. No French driver won another Grand Prix until Pierre Gasly at Monza in 2020.
