On this day: Adams and Lotterer, Spa's one-off F1 drivers, born

Two drivers born on 19 November made brief F1 appearances at Spa: Belgium's Philippe Adams in 1994 and Le Mans winner André Lotterer in 2014.

By Ayush Garg

Two drivers born on 19 November had F1 careers that began at the same place: the Belgian Grand Prix at Spa-Francorchamps.

1969: Philippe Adams is born

Philippe Adams was born in Mouscron, Belgium. He won the British Formula Two Championship in 1993, after finishing second in British Formula 3 the year before.

In 1994 he and his backers agreed to find $500,000 for two races with Team Lotus. Part of the money came from an insurance policy against his win in a Belgian Procar race at Spa.

His debut came at his home Grand Prix at Spa on 28 August 1994. He qualified 26th, 12.722 seconds off Rubens Barrichello's pole time, and spun out on lap 15.

He finished 16th in Portugal in his second race, then lost the seat back to Alessandro Zanardi and never raced in F1 again.

1981: André Lotterer is born

André Lotterer was born in Duisburg, in what was then West Germany. By the time he reached F1 he had won the Le Mans 24 Hours outright for Audi in 2011 and the 2012 World Endurance Championship.

Caterham gave him a one-off drive at the 2014 Belgian Grand Prix on 24 August, in place of Kamui Kobayashi. He qualified 21st, almost a second quicker than team-mate Marcus Ericsson.

His race lasted a single lap. An electrical failure stopped him, after a loss of power that may have come from a fuse knocked loose over a kerb at Blanchimont.

Lotterer went on to race in Formula E from 2017 to 2023 and won a second World Endurance title with Porsche in 2024.
