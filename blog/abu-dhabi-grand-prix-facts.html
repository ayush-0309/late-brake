Six F1 Abu Dhabi Grand Prix facts that sound made up

A hotel over the track, a pit exit through a tunnel and a champion who led for the first time at the flag: six true facts about F1 in Abu Dhabi.

By Ayush Garg

The 2026 F1 season ends at the Yas Marina Circuit in Abu Dhabi, a week after Qatar. Here are six true facts about the place that sound unlikely.

1. It hosted F1's first day-night race

The first Abu Dhabi Grand Prix, on 1 November 2009, started at 5pm local time, in daylight, and finished in the dark.

The floodlights were on from the start, so the cars raced through sunset into night. It was the first F1 race to do so.

2. The track runs under a hotel

The cars pass beneath the W Abu Dhabi, Yas Island hotel, which is built over the circuit.

3. The pit exit goes through a tunnel

Leaving the pits at Yas Marina means driving under the circuit. Part of the pit lane exit dips into a tunnel that passes beneath the track.

Not every driver liked it: Giancarlo Fisichella was one who criticised the design.

4. The track is made of English stone

The asphalt uses greywacke stone shipped from a quarry in Shropshire, England.

Drivers praise the grip it gives, but it also wears tyres harder.

5. Vettel led the 2010 title race for the first time at the flag

Sebastian Vettel arrived at the 2010 finale third in the standings, 15 points behind Fernando Alonso. He had not led the championship at any point all season.

He won from pole while Alonso was stuck behind Vitaly Petrov and finished seventh. Vettel took the title by four points, 256 to 252, and became the first driver since James Hunt in 1976 to lead the standings for the first time at the end of the final race.

At 23 years and 134 days, he was also the youngest champion in F1 history.

6. Hamilton's perfect finale

In 2019 Lewis Hamilton started from pole, led every lap and set the fastest lap, F1's "grand slam". He won by 16.772 seconds from Max Verstappen.

It made him the first driver to score points in every race of a season twice, after doing it in 2017 too.
