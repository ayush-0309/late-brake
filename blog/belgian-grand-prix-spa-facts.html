Six F1 Belgian Grand Prix facts that sound made up

A first race with one entry, twenty wet years in a row and a Grand Prix of two laps: six true stories from the Belgian Grand Prix and Spa.

By Ayush Garg

Spa-Francorchamps is one of the most loved tracks in F1, and the Belgian Grand Prix has some of its oddest stories. Here are six that are true.

1. The first race was called off because only one driver entered

The circuit's first race was planned for August 1921. It was cancelled when just one driver signed up.

The first Grand Prix at Spa was held four years later, in 1925.

2. It once rained at the race twenty years in a row

Spa is famous for its weather, and at one stage the Belgian Grand Prix was wet for twenty straight years.

Drivers can still find one part of the track dry and bright while another stretch is soaked.

3. The original lap was more than 14 kilometres long

The old Spa measured more than 14 km a lap. In 1979 it was cut to less than half that length.

Even so, today's lap is still 7.004 km, with 19 turns.

4. Jackie Stewart was trapped upside down in fuel

In the rain at Spa in 1966, more than half the field crashed out on the first lap. Jackie Stewart's BRM went off at the Masta Kink, hit a telephone pole and landed in a ditch.

He was stuck upside down for more than 20 minutes with fuel pooling in the cockpit, until Graham Hill and Bob Bondurant freed him with a spectator's toolkit. The crash started Stewart's long campaign for safer racing.

5. One driver won every race held at Nivelles

When Spa lost the race on safety grounds, Belgium moved it between Zolder and a circuit at Nivelles near Brussels. Nivelles hosted only two World Championship races, in 1972 and 1974.

Emerson Fittipaldi won both, the first for Lotus and the second for McLaren, beating Niki Lauda by 0.35 seconds in 1974.

6. The 2021 race lasted two laps behind the safety car

Heavy rain delayed the 2021 race for more than three hours. When it finally got going, the cars completed two laps behind the safety car before a red flag on lap three.

The result was counted back to the end of lap one, a distance of 6.880 km, and half points were awarded. Max Verstappen won the shortest World Championship race in history without a single green-flag lap.
