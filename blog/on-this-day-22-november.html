On this day: Kubica's F1 comeback and Verstappen's Vegas win

On 22 November, Williams confirmed Robert Kubica's return to F1 eight years after his rally crash, and Verstappen won in Las Vegas as both McLarens were disqualified.

By Ayush Garg

Two moments from 22 November: one of the sport's great comebacks, and a Las Vegas result decided after the flag.

2018: Williams confirms Kubica's return

Williams announced that Robert Kubica would race for the team in 2019, alongside the rookie George Russell. The news came days before the 2018 season finale in Abu Dhabi.

It completed a comeback eight years after a rally crash nearly ended his career. Kubica had been preparing for a second season with Renault when he crashed on the Ronde di Andora rally in northern Italy in February 2011.

He was badly hurt, including serious injuries to his right arm, and spent the next years rallying and testing other cars. He returned to F1 machinery in tests with Renault in 2017, and drove in three practice sessions for Williams in 2018.

"Nothing is impossible," Kubica told the BBC on the day. In 2019 he scored a point at the German Grand Prix, his first since 2010.

2025: Verstappen wins as McLaren loses both cars

Max Verstappen won the Las Vegas Grand Prix for Red Bull, 23.546 seconds ahead of George Russell's Mercedes. It was his second win on the Strip.

Lando Norris had started from pole and finished second on the road, with team-mate Oscar Piastri fourth. Both McLarens were then disqualified because the plank under their cars had worn down too far.

That promoted Russell to second and his team-mate Kimi Antonelli to third. Antonelli had started 17th and served a five-second penalty for a jump start.

The result left Verstappen level with Piastri on 366 points, 24 behind the championship leader Norris, with two races to go.
